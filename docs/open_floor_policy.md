# Open-floor discussion and language policy (design draft)

This draft supersedes the **discussion** part of the implemented v0.4 rules.
The current engine still schedules three speaking slots per survivor. Saved
v0.4 games, training reports, and win rates remain measurements of that old
game; they are not benchmarks for the redesigned discussion.

## Boundary between referee and agents

The Godfather keeps hidden roles, legal actions, speaking budgets, public
history, night and vote resolution, and the floor-control rule. It does not
judge whether a claim is persuasive or select information for a player to
share. Each agent sees its own role and private events plus the same public
conversation and chooses its own action.

During discussion, the policy must be able to choose:

- Whether to seek the floor now or wait for new information.
- Whether to speak, yield, or attempt to overpower another speaker when legal.
- The exact public message, including how much private information to expose,
  whether to claim a role, whom to accuse, and when to stop.

The message is generated by the agent. The environment only validates the
action and the length limit; it does not substitute a fixed Detective reveal
sentence. Mafia can make false claims. Town can reveal fully, hint, challenge,
or remain quiet. Those possibilities are examples for analysis, not a menu of
prewritten messages that the policy must select from.

## Confirmed floor rule

After each public discussion event, every living agent with speaking capacity
gets the same public snapshot and independently submits a sealed choice:
`wait` or `speak(message)`. The message is a proposed utterance. The Godfather
collects choices sequentially for OpenEnv transport but reveals none until the
window closes, so collection order gives no one an informational advantage.
If several agents request the floor, a seeded equal-chance draw selects the
speaker. Unselected proposals are discarded without consuming their voice.
There is no predetermined speaker cycle, and a player is never required to
speak.

Before the selected message is published, the Godfather announces who won the
floor. Other eligible agents may `allow` or `overpower` that speaker with their
own replacement message; an empty replacement is legal. Every agent that
attempts overpowering spends one speaking credit, as requested in the original
rule. If several attempt it, a second seeded equal-chance draw selects whose
replacement message is published. The original speaker loses its selected
speaking credit whether or not it is interrupted. No chain of interruptions
is allowed in this first version. A public event records the successful
overpower; losing attempts need not expose a private draft message.

If no agent requests the floor in a complete window, discussion ends and voting
begins. A speaking budget also bounds self-play so one agent cannot prevent a
vote forever. Whether the old three turns become a per-player **maximum of
three accepted speaking actions** or we use one shared day limit is the next
design choice. A limit must never force anyone to speak. The later power trait
can alter floor priority through an explicit rule; initial agents have equal
priority. Guaranteed day elimination, runoff, night actions, role privacy, and
terminal team reward remain as they are unless separately changed.

## Learnable policy

The policy is a distribution over legal actions **and generated message
tokens** given the active agent's observation and public history. Requesting
the floor and deciding what to say are both policy decisions. A Detective's
investigation answer stays private until its own message exposes some or all
of it. No fixed reveal timing or fixed reveal wording is part of the rules.

The first training boundary can update one Town policy against varied frozen
Mafia opponents, while all agents still follow the same environment contract.
Terminal Town win/loss remains the optimization reward. Diagnostics may label
claims, reveal timing, interruptions, vote shifts, and who won the floor, but
those labels must not become a hidden referee signal in the agent observation
or a reward for revealing itself.

## Implementation consequences

1. Replace `env/rules.py`'s scheduled speaking turns and `discussion_round`
   with a floor-request state and a public discussion event index.
2. Extend OpenEnv action and observation types for waiting, floor requests,
   floor grants, and interruption resolution.
3. Update the LLM action parser, scripted opponents, and rollout controller.
4. Version the ruleset so v0.4 traces and adapters cannot be silently mixed
   with new evaluations.
5. Test optional silence, variable speaking order, overpower cost, guaranteed
   transition to voting, privacy, and reproducibility from a seed.

## Decision before implementation

Set the speaking-budget rule: a per-player cap of three accepted speech actions
per day, or a shared day event limit. In either case, silence is always legal
and a completely quiet request window moves the game to voting.
